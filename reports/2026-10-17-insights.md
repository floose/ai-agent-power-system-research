## 1. Residual correction under weather shifts

Li et al. [1] combine a large temporal model with a lightweight, attention-gated gradient-boosting residual model. The study addresses weather-sensitive grid demand using hourly data and a 24-hour horizon. Table 5 reports MAE of 19.6 MW versus 31.8 MW for the standalone large model; RMSE is 28.5 versus 45.6 MW. These are study-specific retrospective results, not independent reproduction or measured dispatch savings. Relevance lies in adapting forecasts without repeatedly updating the entire large model. Data-access and leakage controls require closer scrutiny before operational adoption.

**Evidence:** publisher HTML, Sec. 2.2, Sec. 4.1–4.3, Tables 2, 3 and 5. Coverage: full-text evidence reviewed. Peer-review and publication dates are shown on the publisher page; a separate correction/retraction database check was not completed.

## 2. Graph forecasting with heuristic hyperparameter tuning

Ji et al. [2] couple a temporal graph convolutional network with an improved salp swarm optimizer. Graph learning models spatiotemporal load dependence; the heuristic tunes hyperparameters. This distinction matters: the method combines learning and search rather than treating them as interchangeable. The publisher marks the source as an early peer-reviewed accepted article, subject to replacement by the final Version of Record. Only the abstract was accessible for this reading. Quantitative performance, comparative superiority, split design and generalization are therefore left unassessed pending full-text review.

**Evidence:** publisher Abstract, version notice and About this article. Coverage: abstract only; do not treat this as completed scientific appraisal.

## Interpretation

For this small selection, an appropriate review distinction is between **forecast adaptation** [1] and **architecture/hyperparameter design** [2]. Neither establishes better grid operation solely by reporting lower prediction errors. This is editorial interpretation, not a literature-wide trend.
